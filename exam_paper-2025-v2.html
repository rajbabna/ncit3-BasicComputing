Title: Cyclone Chido Devastates Mayotte – December 2024

On 14 December 2024, Cyclone Chido, one of the most powerful tropical storms to strike the Indian Ocean in nearly a century, made direct landfall on the French overseas territory of Mayotte. The cyclone brought sustained winds exceeding 220 km/h, causing catastrophic damage across the archipelago and triggering a large-scale humanitarian crisis.

The disaster resulted in at least 39 confirmed deaths, though authorities warned the true toll could be significantly higher given the destruction of densely populated informal settlements. More than 100,000 people were left without shelter, and critical infrastructure including hospitals, schools, and the main water distribution network was severely disrupted.

The French government declared a state of natural disaster and dispatched emergency relief teams within 24 hours. The response highlighted longstanding concerns about infrastructure vulnerability in France's overseas territories and the growing intensity of climate-related extreme weather events in the region.
